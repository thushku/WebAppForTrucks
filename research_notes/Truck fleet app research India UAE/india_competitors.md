## 1. Who is the target user, and what is the business model of each app?

### Takeaway
The Indian market splits into four groups. (a) **Hardware/telematics-led platforms** (WheelsEye, BlackBuck, LocoNav, Fleetx, Intangles, FleetRobo, Vamosys, Mappls) earn most of their revenue from GPS devices, subscriptions, FASTag and fuel. (b) **Free or cheap "khata" (ledger) apps** (TransportBook, Truck Khata, Truck Trip Hisab) are aimed at small owners and keep the owner's own books. (c) **A new wave of 2025–26 SMB trip-P&L apps** (Trucksarthi, Fleetbooks, FleetHisab) adds driver apps, OCR and compliance. (d) **Transport ERPs and Tally add-ons** serve transporters and 3PLs that issue LRs and bill customers. Nobody in the top tier is software-only for small owners. The software-only players are small or new.

### Inferences
- The big, funded players (WheelsEye at ₹243 Cr revenue, BlackBuck at ₹652 Cr, Fleetx at about ₹80 Cr) all depend on **hardware plus FASTag/fuel/payment** revenue. None of them is primarily a trip-accounting product. Their incentive is to sell devices and payment flows, not to keep crew-level books.
- Small-owner accounting is served mainly by **free or cheap ledger apps** with thin teams (TransportBook, Truck Khata) and by **very new (2025–26) entrants** (Trucksarthi, Fleetbooks, FleetHisab). This suggests the "trip P&L + driver app" niche is only now getting crowded. The window is open but closing.
- Transport ERPs (VCS, Fleetable, BharatFleet) target transporters/3PLs who issue LRs and bill shippers, not owner-operators with 1–20 trucks.

## 2. Feature coverage per app (trips, driver app, expenses, ledgers, LR/POD, GST, accounting, maintenance, tyres, documents, fuel theft, driver behaviour, tracking source, offline, languages)

### Takeaway
The telematics players are strong on tracking, fuel sensors, alerts and driver behaviour, and have some trip, expense and tyre modules (Fleetx has the deepest). They are weak on owner-level books, crew ledgers and offline crew data capture. The khata apps are strong on party, supplier and driver ledgers, LR/bilty and P&L, but most data is typed by the owner: they have no crew app, no photo proof and no GPS stamps. Only the 2025–26 entrants (Trucksarthi, FleetHisab, Fleetbooks) combine a driver or OCR capture flow with a ledger and P&L.

### Inferences
- **The clearest gaps across all apps:** (1) crew-team modelling on a trip (driver plus co-driver plus helper, each with their own ledger); (2) leave and bonus tracking; (3) phone-based background GPS with photo-plus-GPS-stamped evidence of odometer, fuel and pump readings, recorded by the crew; (4) police/RTO and weighbridge expense categories with proof. I found no evidence that any competitor documents these. That is not proof that they are absent.
- Fleetx is the only incumbent whose documentation shows both receipt-photo AI and advance-versus-expense settlement, but it targets enterprises (2,000+ clients such as Adani and Unilever) and depends on hardware.
- The khata apps are owner-typed ledgers. Data quality depends on the owner re-keying what drivers report by phone. Crew-captured data with proof is the differentiator.
- Offline capture is documented only by FleetHisab (owner-side). This is a real gap for highway crew apps.
- No Tally export was found from any SMB app. That could be a wedge for transporters whose CA works in Tally.
- Fuel theft detection today relies on hardware (WheelsEye diesel sensor, LocoNav, Vamosys, Intangles). A software-only product could use pump receipt plus odometer plus GPS reconciliation as a "soft" fuel audit (fuel per km per trip against truck norms). This is an inference, not a sourced claim.

## 5. Tech approach (native vs hybrid, hardware, tracking source)

### Takeaway
Tracking in the market today is overwhelmingly **hardware-based** (wired or OBD GPS, AIS-140 units, CAN-bus readers, diesel sensors, dashcams, immobiliser relays). Phone-based tracking appears only as TransportBook's "SIM tracking" (network-based). Whether apps are native or hybrid could not be determined from public sources.

### Cited Findings
- **Fleetx:** AIS-140 devices, supplied, fitted and Vahan-linked by the platform vendor. — [Fleetx blog](https://blog.fleetx.ai/blog-ais-140-gps-device-price-in-india-2026/)

- **LocoNav:** wired and wireless-OBD GPS, AIS-140 truck units and dashcams. — [LocoNav](https://loconav.com/gps-tracker); [IndiaMART](https://www.indiamart.com/proddetail/loconav-vehicle-tracking-solution-gps-device-19976022812.html); [Dash cam](https://expenses.loconav.com/dash-cam)

### Inferences
- **Hardware compliance:** If AIS-140 devices are mandatory on the owner's trucks, many owners will already have a hardware tracker. A software platform that **ingests existing tracker feeds** (as Mappls does via API) alongside phone GPS could avoid competing on hardware.
- **Phone-based tracking:** Background phone GPS from the crew app is effectively unoccupied ground among the documented competitors. Its main risks are battery drain, OS background limits and drivers switching phones off.

**General market observation**
- A 2026 roundup notes that neither Fleetx's nor LocoNav's public pages confirm multilingual support, offline/low-connectivity operation or GST integration. — [Bouncedaily](https://www.bouncedaily.in/feeds/blog/best-fleet-management-software)
- Fleetbooks argues that "most tools show revenue but not profit per trip" **[competitor claim]**. — [Fleetbooks blog](https://fleetbooks.in/blog/best-fleet-transport-management-software-india-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Support is the moat nobody has.** Every hardware vendor's complaints centre on after-sales service, installation and technician visits. A software-only product with no installation step avoids the biggest complaint category entirely. It must still staff responsive (WhatsApp/Hindi) support, because khata apps get hit for unresponsive support too.
- **Data portability:** TransportBook users complain about export. Excel, PDF and Tally export from day one, including on lower tiers, is a cheap differentiator.
- **Pricing trust:** Paywalling features users already had (TransportBook) causes backlash. Pricing tiers should be clear and stable.
- **Alert reliability** (false ignition-off, delayed notifications) erodes trust in telematics. Any phone-GPS alerts we ship must be conservative.

- No complaints data for Intangles, Vamosys, FleetRobo, Mappls or TrucksUp.
