# Crew Phones Can Win Truck Accounting

No app we found in India, the UAE or anywhere else does what our product plans to do: let the driver, co-driver and helper each record every road expense on their own phone, with GPS, real time and photo proof even without signal, and turn those entries into per-person ledgers, per-trip profit and books that meet the law. India's biggest players earn from GPS devices and their subscriptions, FASTag and fuel rather than from crew-level books: BlackBuck reported ₹652 Cr revenue in FY26 ([ScanX](https://scanx.trade/stock-market-news/companies/blackbuck-limited-schedules-board-meeting-on-may-19-2026-to-approve-fy26-financial-results/40151145)), WheelsEye ₹243 Cr in FY25 ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/unlisted-companies/wheelseye-reports-modest-revenue-growth-losses-remain-steady-in-fy25)) and Fleetx about ₹80 Cr ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/fleetx/)). Free "khata" (ledger) apps such as TransportBook, with 1M+ installs, depend on the owner typing in what drivers report by phone. The closest look-alikes (Trucksarthi, Fleetbooks, FleetHisab) launched only in 2025–26, and none models helpers, leave, bonus, driver changes, or unreceipted payments with two-person approval. The global leaders Samsara and Motive charge roughly USD 25–94 per truck per month plus hardware on three-year contracts, while software-only Fleetio charges USD 4–10; in the UAE, mandatory SecurePath (Dubai) and Asateel (Abu Dhabi) trackers mean we must work alongside the compulsory box, not replace it. Our edge is therefore **proof, people and proper books**, sold with no hardware and no lock-in. The timing helps: India now requires an audit trail that cannot be switched off, has a new Income-tax Act from 1 April 2026 and new Labour Codes from 21 November 2025, and brings in data-protection duties from May 2027, while the UAE adds mandatory e-invoicing in 2027, salary payment by the 1st of the month from June 2026, and VAT on Salik tolls from June 2026. Every fact here comes from web-search summaries, because page fetches were blocked and the search budget ran out, so re-check prices, legal thresholds and competitor features before relying on them, and confirm every tax and labour item with a Chartered Accountant (CA) in India and a registered tax agent in the UAE.

## How far to trust these findings

Six research notes were compiled on 13 October 2026, covering Indian competitors, UAE and global competitors, Indian law, UAE law, phone and GPS technology, and day-to-day transport practice. In every case the tool that opens web pages failed (DNS errors, proxy refusals), and the shared search budget ran out before the last planned searches. **Every fact here is what a search engine's summary said a cited page contained; nobody read the full page.** Many competitor facts are the vendors' own marketing. Where sources disagree (Fleetx revenue, WheelsEye truck counts, Darb toll caps, the GST Annexure V deadline), the report says so.

Some topics were not researched at all: UAE fuel cards (ADNOC in particular), UAE domestic waybill practice, typical Indian bata, hamali and "mamool" amounts, and tyre position-coding standards. No competitor app was installed and tested. Field lists marked as "practice" come from experienced-practitioner knowledge in the notes and must be checked in interviews with real transporters. Before building, the four most useful next steps are a hands-on test of Trucksarthi (owner and driver apps), TransportBook, Fleetx Driver and WheelsEye; a demo of Truckoom in Dubai; ten to fifteen interviews with owners of 1–50 trucks in India; and a review of section 7 by a CA and a UAE tax agent.

## 1. Competitor analysis: hardware sellers dominate, and nobody keeps the crew's books

### India splits into four camps, and only the newest one aims at our customer

Indian truck software falls into four groups, and seeing why each exists explains the gap we can fill.

**The first group is the hardware and payments platforms**: WheelsEye, BlackBuck, Fleetx, LocoNav and Intangles, which earn mainly from GPS boxes, FASTag (India's electronic toll tag) and fuel. WheelsEye made **₹152.7 Cr of its ₹243 Cr FY25 revenue from software subscriptions sold with its GPS devices**, plus ₹62 Cr from the devices themselves, and still lost about ₹47 Cr ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/unlisted-companies/wheelseye-reports-modest-revenue-growth-losses-remain-steady-in-fy25)). BlackBuck is now listed and profitable (₹160 Cr profit in FY26); its FASTags are linked to about **40% of India's truck tolling** ([JM Financial](https://jmflresearch.com/JMnew/JMCRM/analystreports/pdf/Zinka_IC_2Feb25.pdf)), and its app sells loads, FASTag, GPS and diesel cashback ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.zinka.boss&hl=en_IN)). In neither company's material did researchers find a trip-expense ledger or a driver khata. One WheelsEye customer story describes an owner who **takes kilometre figures out of the app to calculate driver salaries by hand** ([WheelsEye LinkedIn](https://in.linkedin.com/company/wheelseye)), which is the gap in one sentence.

Fleetx is the most complete of this group. Its driver app photographs fuel and toll receipts, AI reads the amount, and a trip settlement nets the driver's advance against fuel, toll and detention costs ([Fleetx blog](https://blog.fleetx.ai/from-settlement-delays-to-seamless-payouts-how-enterprise-fleets-are-automating-trip-settlement-at-scale/)). It also has a tyre module and electronic proof of delivery (e-POD). But Fleetx sells AIS-140 GPS devices and serves "over 2,000 clients, including Adani Group and Unilever" ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/fleetx/)), so it is an enterprise product, not a tool for a five-truck owner. LocoNav sells GPS units, dashcams and driver-monitoring cameras in "15+ languages", but its recent Play review average has reportedly fallen to **1.20 against an all-time 4.51** ([chrome-stats](https://chrome-stats.com/d/com.loconav/reviews)).

**The second group is the free or cheap khata apps**, which keep the owner's own books. TransportBook has **1M+ installs** and a 4.6 rating on Google Play ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=in.transportbook.app&hl=en_US&gl=IN)). Its free Basic plan covers trips, party, supplier and driver ledgers, LR/bilty (the lorry receipt) and "SIM tracking", and Premium costs **₹4,999 a year** ([transportbook.in](https://transportbook.in/)). Truck Khata and Truck Trip Hisab are free. These apps handle the commercial side well (freight, advance, brokerage), but the owner types everything in from phone calls with the driver, with no photos, GPS or time stamps from the road.

**The third group, the 2025–26 wave of small-fleet trip-profit apps, contains our closest rivals.** Trucksarthi was founded in 2025. It has an owner app and a free driver app covering driver khata (daily expenses, salary and advances), bilty, GST invoices, trip accounts and document reminders, for **₹1,799 for 3 months or ₹5,999 a year** on its website ([trucksarthi.in](https://trucksarthi.in/)), and it describes itself as "an operational record-keeping tool… not accounting software" ([Trucksarthi About](https://trucksarthi.in/about)). Fleetbooks charges **₹99 per truck per month** and reads photos of handwritten Tamil or Hindi trip sheets ([Fleetbooks blog](https://fleetbooks.in/blog/best-fleet-transport-management-software-india-2026/)). FleetHisab starts at **₹299 a month** ([FleetHisab About](https://fleethisab.in/about)); it reads fuel and toll receipts on the phone with no internet, pulls PUC, fitness, insurance and permit expiry dates from the government's Vahan database, and sends WhatsApp reminders ([fleethisab.in](https://fleethisab.in/)).

**The fourth group is transport ERPs and Tally add-ons**, which serve booking agents and 3PLs who issue LRs and bill shippers. VCS claims "500+ clients since 1990" ([vcs-software.com](https://vcs-software.com/)), BharatFleet starts at **₹30,000 a year** ([Capterra India](https://www.capterra.in/software/1079597/BharatFleet)), and Tally add-ons such as an LR management module cost **₹9,000** ([Tally TDL Store](https://tallytdlstore.com/index.php/product/tally-tdl-for-lr-management/)). One outsider is worth watching: Raddar lets drivers log trips and expenses over **WhatsApp in English, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada and Malayalam** ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/raddar)), which shows how much easy local-language input matters.

Across all four groups, researchers found no app that puts a co-driver and a helper on a trip as separate people with their own ledgers, tracks leave or bonus, lets the crew record odometer, pump-meter and receipt photos with GPS stamps, or has police/RTO and weighbridge expense categories with proof. **Offline entry is documented only by FleetHisab**, and no small-fleet app documents a Tally export. "Not found" is not proof that a feature is absent, but the pattern holds across all eleven products compared.

### The UAE has a compulsory tracker, one giant marketplace and a few local TMS vendors

The UAE market has a different shape, because the law already puts a box in every heavy truck. Vendor material says Dubai's **SecurePath** (run by SIRA with the RTA) covers heavy vehicles from 2.5 tonnes ([IOTee](https://iotee.ae/blog/securepath-asateel-mandatory-gps-tracking-compliance-uae/)), and that Abu Dhabi's ITC requires vehicles to be linked to its **Asateel** platform, with an approved device reportedly needed to issue or renew permits ([Permits.ae](https://permits.ae/asateel-and-securepath-premium-uae-transport/)). That creates a low-cost "compliance bundle" tier: **IOTee**, for example, sells hardware, installation, software and SecurePath/Asateel paperwork for **AED 500 in year one and AED 400 a year after** ([IOTee](https://iotee.ae/best-gps-tracking-companies-uae/)).

Truckoom is the closest UAE look-alike to us: a TMS with quotations, trip planning, a driver app, expense tracking and a document vault, sold alongside its own SIRA-approved SecurePath tracker ([Truckoom Core TMS](https://truckoom.com/core-tms/); [Truckoom SecurePath](https://truckoom.com/securepath-premium-services/)). Fleetroot in Dubai had 100+ clients in the UAE and Saudi Arabia in 2022 ([Entrepreneur Middle East](https://mena.entrepreneur.com/starting-a-business/startup-spotlight-dubai-based-fleetroot-is-easing/428956)). The giant is **TruKKer**, a freight marketplace connecting **60,000+ transporters with 1,200+ enterprise clients in 9 countries**, which raised USD 15M in July 2025 ([Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2025/07/trukker-raises-15-million-debt-ruya-partners)). Its free Partner app already lets small carriers add trucks and drivers, track trips, see earnings per trip and upload PODs, in **Arabic, Hindi, Urdu and Punjabi** ([TruKKer Partner](https://www.trukker.com/trukker-partner)). No researcher found crew ledgers, or fuel, toll and weighbridge capture, in it.

Global brands (Webfleet, MiX/Powerfleet with its oil-and-gas fleets, Cartrack, and Geotab via GoFleet) are sold through local resellers. Accounting is done in **Zoho Books**, whose Standard plan includes VAT filing from AED 69 a month, or in **TallyPrime** ([Sarmat](https://sarmat.ae/resources/accounting-software-used-in-uae)). Fleetx's owner app has a UAE Play listing with 100K+ downloads ([Google Play AE](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.fleetx&hl=en_US&gl=AE)), but researchers found no other evidence of Fleetx operating in the UAE.

### Global leaders sell boxes on three-year contracts; software-only tools sell maintenance or US back-office

The hardware-led group (Samsara, Motive, Geotab, Webfleet and Verizon Connect) publishes no rate cards. A competitor's analysis of Samsara's public cooperative price sheet puts it at **USD 39 per vehicle per month for the gateway plus USD 55 for an AI dashcam**, with hardware at USD 99–548, on a **3-year minimum** ([Airpinpoint](https://airpinpoint.com/compare/samsara-pricing)). Motive is estimated at **USD 25–50 per vehicle per month** plus hardware ([Levy Fleets](https://fleets.levyelectric.com/compare/motive-pricing)), and reviewers report three-year, non-cancellable, auto-renewing contracts ([Spotsaas](https://www.spotsaas.com/blog/motive-review-trucking-fleet-management)).

These products are excellent at live tracking, safety cameras and US hours-of-service logs. Motive lets **up to 9 co-drivers share one device** and switch the active driver only while the truck is stopped ([Motive Help](https://helpcenter.gomotive.com/hc/en-us/articles/30898054391965-Switching-Drivers)); its AI reads fuel receipts ([Motive Help](https://helpcenter.gomotive.com/hc/en-us/articles/6162542840093-Driver-App-Documents)); and Geotab compares fuel-card purchases with GPS to flag fraud ([Geotab](https://www.geotab.com/fleet-management-solutions/fleet-fuel-management/)). But their "co-driver" exists only to meet US driving-hour rules, not to track who spent what, and none of them models a helper.

In the software-only group, **Fleetio** publishes prices of **USD 4, 7 and 10 per vehicle per month** ([Fleetio pricing](https://www.fleetio.com/pricing)). It is the best maintenance model to copy: fuel entries require an odometer reading and are auto-voided if it is out of sequence ([Fleetio](https://help.fleetio.com/en_US/auto-void-fuel-entry)), tyre fields are generated from each truck's axle layout, and failed inspection items become repair issues ([Fleetio Inspections](https://help.fleetio.com/en_US/maintenance/inspections-overview)). TruckingOffice and TruckLogics sell US small-fleet back-office tools on monthly fleet bands of about **USD 25–250** ([Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/truckingoffice/pricing); [FitGap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/024601/trucklogics)).

### What users complain about shows us where to win

Complaints fall into five clear groups, and each one points to a promise we can keep.

**Hardware sellers fail on after-sales service.** WheelsEye users describe a GPS unit still not installed four days after payment, and complaints closed without a technician visit ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.wheelseyeoperator&hl=en_IN)); its iPhone app is rated 3.1 from 660 ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/wheelseye-fastag-gps-fuel/id1600637178)). BlackBuck users report trackers that stopped working for days and FASTag money deducted but not showing ([PissedConsumer](https://blackbuck.pissedconsumer.com/review.html)). Fleetx's iPhone reviews mention crashes after updates and unanswered complaints ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/1532313154?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)).

**Global leaders fail on contracts.** One Verizon Connect customer says a cancellation request sent five months before renewal was ignored, after which they were told they owed **over USD 30,000** ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/verizonconnect.com)). More than 75% of reviewers who rated Webfleet's support called it unhelpful or slow ([SelectHub](https://www.selecthub.com/p/fleet-tracking-software/webfleet/)), and Samsara reviewers object to three-year lock-ins and to standard features being turned into paid add-ons ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/167543/Samsara-for-Fleets-0-00-6-23/reviews/)).

**Khata apps fail on data freedom.** One TransportBook user writes, "You never can download your trips neither in excel nor in pdf", and another says features stopped working until they upgraded ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=in.transportbook.app&hl=en_US&gl=IN)).

**Small-fleet tools fail on reliability.** TruckLogics reviews cite crashes and data loss, and TruckingOffice settlements show the driver's pay without the load breakdown ([SoftwareConnect](https://softwareconnect.com/reviews/truckingoffice/)).

**Surveillance creates legal risk.** A Samsara biometric class action is listed as settled ([ClassAction.org](https://www.classaction.org/news/lawsuit-claims-samsara-collects-truck-drivers-biometric-info-without-proper-disclosures-consent)), and in July 2026 drivers filed a proposed class action alleging, so far without any finding, that Motive's dashcams act as licence-plate readers ([The Auto Wire](https://theautowire.com/2026/08/01/motive-ai-dashcam-lawsuit-alpr-truckers/)).

Taken together, these give us five promises to make and keep: no box to install, no long contract, export on every plan, an app that never loses an entry, and tracking only while on duty.

## 2. Our edge: proof, people and proper books, with no box to install

Our advantages come straight from the decisions already agreed with the founder (recorded in `docs/00-decisions.md`), and each one targets a gap the research found.

**Proof recorded by the person who spent the money.** Every crew entry carries the phone's GPS position, the real time of the event, and photos: odometer, pump meter and receipt for fuel; the slip for a weighbridge; the receipt or challan for official fines. If there is no signal, the entry is kept on the phone with its actual time and sent automatically when signal returns (D12).

> **Example.** Suresh fills 180 litres at 2:10 a.m. on a ghat section near Kasara with no network. The entry, the pump's GPS point and three photos sit safely on his phone and reach the office at 2:55 a.m., when the truck finds signal. Nothing is re-typed, and nothing can be backdated.

TransportBook and Truck Khata, by contrast, rely on the owner re-typing phone calls. Fleetx captures receipt photos but documents no odometer or pump-meter photos and no offline mode, and FleetHisab works offline only on the owner's side. Samsara's document forms lose their contents if the app closes before submission ([Samsara KB](https://kb.samsara.com/hc/en-us/articles/360006617072-Submit-and-View-Documents)), which is exactly what our design must avoid.

**The whole crew, as people with their own money trail.** A trip has a truck, a driver, a co-driver and a helper (A1), and every entry belongs to the person who made it. Each person has a ledger covering advance, bata (daily or trip allowance), salary, bonus, leave, deductions and payments. **No Indian, UAE or global product reviewed models helpers or tracks leave and bonus.** Motive's co-driver feature exists only for US driving-hour logs ([Motive Help](https://helpcenter.gomotive.com/hc/en-us/articles/6162491711389-What-is-Co-Drivers-team-driving-in-the-Driver-App)).

> **Example.** A helper who receives ₹2,000 for food on a Kochi–Mumbai run sees that advance, his own expense entries and his balance on his own phone. At settlement time he no longer depends on the driver's memory.

**An honest way to handle money paid without a receipt.** Practitioner notes list expenses with no bill (police or checkpost payments, cash loading charges, roadside puncture repairs) as the most common cause of disputes at settlement; this is an unverified practice note to confirm in interviews. Our "Payment without receipt" category (P3) needs approval by both drivers (D5). The second approval can come at any time until the trip closes, and the trip cannot be settled while an entry still shows "Waiting for 2nd approval" (D6). The owner can switch the rule on or off (D7), and on a single-driver trip the manager approves instead (A4, a default still to be confirmed). Official fines with a challan or receipt need only the number and a photo (D5). **None of the 20+ competitors reviewed documents anything like this.** It turns a he-said-she-said argument into a recorded, two-signature fact.

**Exact driving hours per driver.** When the person taking the wheel taps "I am driving now", the app records the time, GPS and odometer (D11, P2). That yields driving hours per driver per trip, day and week, with alerts. India has no electronic logbook law; driver hours come from labour law, at 8 hours a day and 48 a week ([Counselence OSH overview](https://counselence.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/CEC-Labour-Code-Overview-of-the-OSH-Code-November-28-2025.pdf)). The UAE has no statutory driving-hours scheme either ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/safety-a-heavy-responsibility?amp=1)). So no local competitor records who was at the wheel, even though it matters for overtime pay, rest alerts, and accident or insurance claims.

**Trip profit that stays trustworthy after the trip.** Each trip shows income, expenses and profit. Once a trip closes, crew entries are locked, but the owner can still add office-side costs such as commission, a late repair bill, or an insurance or EMI share, and every change goes into the audit log (D8). Fleetbooks claims that "most tools show revenue but not profit per trip" ([Fleetbooks](https://fleetbooks.in/blog/best-fleet-transport-management-software-india-2026/)). We go a step further: profit built from proven entries, with a full edit history.

**Real accounting, built in for each country (D10).** Two Indian rules make built-in books more valuable than an export-only approach. Every Indian company must keep its books in software whose **audit trail cannot be switched off**, preserved for **eight years**, with a **daily backup on servers in India** ([India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-mandates-audit-trail-compliance-for-all-companies-explainer-key-obligations-34837.html/); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/company-law/demystifying-audit-trail-edit-log.html)). And GST rules for goods transport agencies changed on 22 September 2025 ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2163560&reg=3&lang=2)). In the UAE, e-invoicing goes live in 2027 and the payroll file format (WPS) is strict. Meanwhile Trucksarthi calls itself "not accounting software", and Fleetio and the global tools send their data out to QuickBooks. A product that runs the books properly, and still exports cleanly to Tally (India) and to Zoho Books or Tally (UAE) because that is where CAs work, has no direct small-fleet rival.

**No hardware, no lock-in, published prices.** Version 1 uses phone GPS (A2), so there is nothing to install and no technician to wait for. That removes the single biggest complaint category for WheelsEye, BlackBuck and LocoNav. Monthly billing, with cancellation inside the app, answers the anger at the three-year contracts of Motive, Samsara, Verizon and Webfleet.

**Small things that matter on a phone call.** Trip numbers such as `261013-ACC-COK-BOM-01` are easy to read aloud (P1), while GST invoices keep their own 16-character series, as the law requires. The apps are built language-ready from day one (open question Q1). TruKKer has already set the UAE bar at Arabic, Hindi, Urdu and Punjabi, and Raddar shows that Indian drivers expect at least six Indian languages.

**What we give up.** A phone cannot match a fuel-level sensor at catching siphoning; it can only run a "soft audit" (section 3). In the UAE, our phone GPS does not replace the legally required SecurePath or Asateel box. And we have no FASTag or fuel-cashback hook to drive installs the way BlackBuck and WheelsEye do. These limits shape the feature plan below.

## 4. Cross-sector appeal: the same building blocks fit buses, tankers, tippers and vans

Underneath the trucking language, the product is a set of reusable building blocks: a **vehicle** with documents and service history; a **crew** of named people with roles; a **job** (we call it a trip) with a route, a start and an end; **evidence-backed entries** of money, meter readings, photos, GPS and time; **per-person ledgers**; **job profit**; and **country-ready accounting and payroll**. Any business that sends a vehicle and a small team out to earn money, and argues afterwards about what was spent, can use it. The meter need not even be an odometer: Fleetio already accepts either "odometer or hour meter" on its fuel entries ([Fleetio](https://help.fleetio.com/using-fleetio/fuel-entry-overview)), so construction machines fit without a redesign.

The sector examples below are our own reasoning, not researched market evidence, and each should be tested with two or three real operators before we market to that sector.

| Sector | Who is the "crew" | What is the "trip" | Special inputs to add | Simple example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Private and intercity bus operators | Driver, relief driver, conductor/cleaner | One scheduled run | Ticket and cash collections as income, seat counts, route permits | A Kochi–Bengaluru sleeper operator with 8 buses sees profit per night run, and the driver-change log shows each driver rested on the overnight leg |
| Fuel, chemical and milk tankers | Driver, helper | One delivery | Seal numbers with photos, quantity at loading and delivery, allowed-loss limit, hazardous-licence expiry | A diesel tanker owner photographs seals at the depot and at the site; a shortage beyond the allowed limit is flagged on the driver's ledger |
| Tippers and quarries | Driver (often one per shift) | Many short trips a day | Weighbridge slip for every load, per-tonne or per-trip pay, trip counter | A quarry owner with 12 tippers pays drivers per trip, counted from slips rather than the driver's notebook |
| School buses | Driver, attendant (the "helper" role) | Morning and evening route | Police-verification and fitness expiry, fee income, a parent-facing location link (with strict privacy) | A school sees each bus's departure time and the attendant's sign-in, and fitness renewals cannot be missed |
| Courier and last-mile vans | Driver, sometimes a helper | A multi-stop route | POD per stop, cash-on-delivery collected, per-drop pay | A van operator matches cash collected at 40 drops against the driver's deposit that evening |
| Construction equipment rental | Operator, helper | A hire period at a site | Hour meter instead of km, diesel issued at the site, daily or hourly rent | A JCB owner bills 9.5 hours a day from photographed hour-meter readings and tracks diesel per hour |
| Retail and FMCG distribution | Driver, salesman | A daily beat | Cash and cheques collected from shops, returns, stock loaded | A distributor's van records ₹1.8 lakh collected from 30 shops, each with a receipt photo and GPS point |

What changes per sector is small: the labels (trip becomes "run", "route" or "hire"), one or two special fields, and the source of income. The approval rules, ledgers, offline capture and accounting stay the same. That argues for configurable labels and per-company custom fields from the start. Fleetio limits custom fields to its higher plans ([Fleetio](https://help.fleetio.com/s/article/Vehicle-Overview)), which suggests customers value them enough to pay.

## 6. Field catalogue: what each screen should capture

Every crew entry, whatever its type, should carry a set of fields that the phone fills in automatically, so the crew never types them and cannot fake them. These are a unique entry ID; who entered it and in what role; the trip and truck; the phone's clock time; a "time since the phone was switched on" counter and a restart marker, which together let the server work out the true event time even if the phone clock was changed; the GPS satellite time; latitude, longitude and accuracy; a fake-GPS flag; whether automatic time is switched on; and the sync status. Section 8 explains the design behind them.

The record-specific fields below combine three sources: legal requirements (cited in section 7), the best competitor patterns (Fleetio's fuel entry, Samsara's defect loop, Fleetx's tyre module), and practitioner knowledge from the operations notes. The practitioner items, such as rate bases, LR payment types, bata models and tyre position codes, are **unverified and must be validated in transporter interviews**.

## 7. Legal and accounting requirements: both countries changed the rules in 2025–26

Both countries changed key rules in the last 18 months, so software designed even a year ago is already out of date. The checklists below set out what the research found, what the software must do, and how sure we are. "Sourced" means a cited page, mostly expert commentary seen through search summaries rather than the official text. "Conflicting" means sources disagree. "Check with CA" or "Check with tax agent" means the point rests on unverified background knowledge, or on a source too thin to build on.

### India checklist

| Topic | What we found | What the software must do | How sure |
|---|---|---|---|

## 8. Technical findings that shape the build

### Android allows all-day GPS only if the driver starts it and a notification stays visible

On Android 14 and later, an app can track location continuously only through a "foreground service" of type *location*: a background task that the phone must show as a permanent notification, and that must be **started while the app is open on screen**, for example when the driver taps "Start duty" ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types); [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start)). The extra "Allow all the time" location permission is needed only if tracking must restart by itself after the phone reboots. Requesting it triggers a Google Play declaration and review, and Google may reject requests "without compelling justification" ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150?hl=en)). Google does allow location in a foreground service that is "initiated as a continuation of an in-app user-initiated action". So the simplest design Play will accept is this: tracking runs only during duty, the driver starts it by tapping "Start duty", and after a reboot a notification asks the driver to tap to resume.

Three further constraints apply. From **31 Aug 2026**, every Play update must target Android 16 (API 36), with extensions possible to 1 Nov 2026 ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk)). Doze mode suspends network access and delays background jobs ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby)). And Xiaomi, Vivo, Oppo, Realme and Samsung phones still stop background apps unless the user changes battery and auto-start settings ([Biolovision](https://help.biolovision.net/Keep_Background_Services_Alive_on_Android_%28by_Brand%29)), which is why the "phone health check" screen and the server's "no GPS for 15 minutes" alert matter.

A sensible setting, based on engineering judgement and to be tuned in a pilot, is one GPS point every 30–60 seconds while moving, sent in batches. That gives roughly 60–120 points an hour and **under 100 KB of data for a 12-hour shift**, in line with Google's advice to use the largest interval possible and to batch updates ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/battery)). Uploads must not use the "data sync" service type, which is capped at 6 hours a day from Android 15 ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-15)).

### iPhones are easier on battery killers but easier for the user to stop

iOS keeps an app tracking in the background if the "Location updates" background mode is on and tracking started in the foreground. If the user swipes the app away, only "significant change" monitoring (moves of about 500 m or more) can wake it again ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/handling-location-updates-in-the-background); [B4X B4i tutorial](https://www.b4x.com/android/forum/threads/background-location-tracking.50246/)). App Store rules require location to be "directly relevant" to the app, consent before collection, and no excessive battery drain ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Apple's newer location APIs are written for Swift, so B4i will likely need a small Objective-C wrapper.

Three pitfalls need planning. ### Offline sync: the phone is the master copy until the server says "got it"

Decision D12 works like this. Every entry gets a unique ID on the phone and goes into a local "outbox" table, storing four kinds of time: the phone clock, a counter of time since the phone was switched on, a restart marker, and the GPS satellite time. A single uploader sends each entry first and its photos afterwards, retrying with growing waits (30 seconds, then 1, 2, 5, 15 and 30 minutes). The server saves each ID once and simply returns its earlier answer if the same entry arrives twice, which is the standard "idempotency key" pattern ([http.dev](https://http.dev/idempotency-key)). Nothing is deleted from the phone until the server confirms it, and a missing photo never blocks the money record. Approvals, such as the second driver's approval, travel as separate commands; the server accepts the first valid one and ignores duplicates. Using the "time since switch-on" counter, the server can rebuild when an event really happened and flag any phone whose clock was moved. That is how a 2 a.m. fuel entry keeps its true time even if the driver later changes the phone clock.

### Anti-fraud: combine many weak signals and never lock out a driver with no signal

No single check is reliable. Android's "fake location" flag "turns out to be unreliable" ([LocationAssistantV2](https://github.com/soniiic/LocationAssistantV2/blob/master/README.md)), and Google's Play Integrity check, the strongest sign of a tampered phone, needs network access and a Play-installed app ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/improvements)). The practical approach is to combine many weak signals and route suspicious entries to the manager. The phone records the fake-location flag, the automatic-time setting and any clock changes. The server checks for impossible speeds between GPS points (for example above 150 km/h for a truck), for fuel entries more than about 300 m from the truck's track or made while the truck had not stopped, and for litres above tank capacity. Photos come only from the in-app camera, with time, GPS, truck and driver burned into the image; a digital fingerprint of each photo, plus a "looks similar" fingerprint on the server, catches the same bill photographed twice. Entries are never blocked from being saved offline; they are flagged for the manager in the approval flow instead.

### Maps and routing: use free map tiles and pay only for the few truck-specific routes

Since March 2025, Google Maps has given **free monthly usage per product** instead of a USD 200 credit: 10,000 calls for Essentials products, 5,000 for Pro and 1,000 for Enterprise, with India-billed accounts getting **35,000 Pro and 7,000 Enterprise** ([Google](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing-categories)). **Google offers no truck routing.** Truck-aware routing comes from HERE, which handles truck height, weight and hazardous-goods settings and has a free plan of 1,000 requests a day ([HERE](https://developer.here.com/pricing)); from Mappls/MapmyIndia, whose directions API has a truck profile ([AWS Marketplace](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-6if2aybofgvqk)); or from self-hosted GraphHopper or OSRM running on free OpenStreetMap data ([GraphHopper forum](https://discuss.graphhopper.com/t/custom-model-for-trucks/7632)). OpenStreetMap often lacks bridge-height and weight-limit data, depending on the region ([OSM Help](https://help.openstreetmap.org/questions/74336/osm-for-commercial-truck-routing)).

The practical choice is to show live positions and trip replays on the web dashboard using free OpenStreetMap tiles (Leaflet or MapLibre); to use Mappls for Indian addresses and Google for UAE addresses within their free limits; to calculate distances and ETAs on a self-hosted OSRM or GraphHopper server; and to pay for HERE or Mappls truck routing only where height or weight limits matter. The crew app needs no turn-by-turn navigation, because drivers already use Google Maps. Mappls and HERE prices could not be confirmed and need a sales call.

### GPS storage at scale: monthly partitions and a one-row "last position" table

Fifty crew phones sending one point every 30–60 seconds for 12 hours a day produce about **36,000–72,000 points a day, or 1–2 million a month**; five hundred phones produce about 20 million a month. MySQL handles this well if the points table is split by month ("range partitioning") and old months are removed with `DROP PARTITION`, which takes milliseconds instead of deleting rows one by one. The trade-off is that partitioned tables cannot use foreign keys, and the timestamp must be part of the primary key ([oneuptime](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-03-31-mysql-use-partitioning-for-data-archival/view)). A small table holding each truck's last position keeps the live map from ever searching millions of rows. After 3–6 months, only simplified trip tracks need to be kept, while the points around fuel and toll events are kept indefinitely as evidence.

Photos go to object storage (an India region for Indian customers, a Middle East region for UAE customers), with only their details held in MySQL. Phones log in with a separate Laravel Sanctum token per device, which can be revoked when a phone is lost ([Context7: Sanctum](https://context7.com/laravel/sanctum)). Indian companies' books need daily backups on servers in India (section 7), and UAE customers should default to UAE hosting because of the data-transfer rules.

## Conclusion

The market splits by who records the data: hardware vendors own the GPS, ledger apps own the owner's notebook, and nobody lets the crew record in a way the owner can trust. So the real moat for this product is **evidence quality and per-person accountability**, not the length of a feature list: a photo with a GPS point and a time that cannot be faked, owned by a named crew member and approved by a second person when there is no receipt. This also reframes the phone-versus-hardware question. A phone cannot beat a diesel sensor at catching siphoning, but a sensor cannot say who paid ₹500 at a checkpost, who was driving at 3 a.m., or whether the helper got his food money, and those are the arguments that cost small owners their time and their crews.

Two forces make the next 18 months unusually favourable, and one risk needs a plan. **First, regulation is pushing small transporters off notebooks and spreadsheets**: in India, an audit trail that cannot be switched off, a locked GSTR-3B, a renumbered Income-tax Act, the Labour Codes and data-protection duties from May 2027; in the UAE, mandatory e-invoicing, stricter WPS and VAT on tolls. A product that turns crew evidence straight into compliant books is selling relief, not software. **Second, mandatory trackers are spreading**, from AIS-140 on Indian national-permit trucks to SecurePath and Asateel in the UAE, so reading those existing boxes later (A2) will matter more than building our own, and in the UAE the right posture towards tracker vendors and TruKKer is partnership, not competition. **The risk is distribution.** BlackBuck and WheelsEye built their large user bases (about 97,000 and 43,000 Play reviews respectively) on FASTag and fuel cashback ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.zinka.boss&hl=en_IN); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.wheelseyeoperator&hl=en_IN)), a hook we will not have. Our substitutes must be built in from day one: a free crew app that each truck's two or three crew members install, WhatsApp-shareable statements that travel from owner to owner, and expiry reminders that keep the app useful even in a week with no trips.
